## Post 7: Before & After

🔄 **6 months ago I didn't know what Prisma was. Today I built a full billing platform with it.**

Here's my honest developer journey:

**Month 1:**
Could barely set up a Next.js project. Had to Google "how to create an API route" every single day. Thought "full stack" meant just frontend + backend.

**Month 2:**
Learned Prisma ORM. Databases went from scary to exciting. Wrote my first complex query and it actually worked. That feeling is unmatched.

**Month 3:**
Built the authentication system. JWT tokens, password hashing, protected routes. Failed 14 times before it worked properly. But when it did — pure magic.

**Month 4:**
Designed the dashboard. Spent 3 days on a single chart. Realized that making data beautiful is harder than making it accurate.

**Month 5:**
Dunning system. Failed payment recovery. Retry logic. This is where I went from "I can code" to "I can think like an engineer."

**Month 6:**
Polished everything. Animations, dark mode, responsive design, search filters. The small details that separate a project from a product.

**The result:**
A billing platform with 12 API endpoints, 15 database models, 16 demo customers, and a dashboard that actually tells a story.

I'm not sharing this to brag. I'm sharing this because if I can do it, so can you.

The secret? Just start. You'll figure out the rest along the way.

## Post 8: What Nobody Tells You

💬 **Nobody told me that building a billing system would teach me more about business than coding.**

When I started **BillFlow**, I thought I was building a software project.

Turns out, I was building a business tool.

**Things I learned that have nothing to do with code:**

📊 **Revenue isn't what you think it is**
MRR, ARR, ARPU — these aren't just acronyms. They're the heartbeat of a SaaS business. Getting them wrong means making bad decisions with confidence.

🔁 **Subscriptions are relationships, not transactions**
A subscription has a lifecycle — trial, activation, renewal, upgrade, downgrade, pause, cancel. Each stage is a conversation with the customer.

⚠️ **Failed payments are opportunities**
When a payment fails, it's not the end — it's a chance to recover the customer. Good dunning logic can save 30%+ of failed payments.

🎯 **Coupons drive behavior**
A well-timed discount isn't just a price cut — it's a psychological trigger. "20% off first 3 months" converts better than "Save $6/month" even though the math is identical.

📱 **UX is the product**
Nobody cares about your clean architecture if the dashboard is confusing. The interface IS the product.

**The biggest lesson?**
Technical skills get you in the door. Business understanding makes you valuable.

I'm grateful this project taught me both.
